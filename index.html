The local advantage
Wisconsin-based. Virtually capable.

Your CMO doesn't have to be in the building every day. But when it matters, she can be.

Business has never been more connected. Your marketing team might work from three different cities. Your agency might be two states away. Your customers might be everywhere. That's a beautiful thing.

But sometimes the most valuable work still happens when people are in the same room: the strategy session that changes the company's direction, the executive meeting before an acquisition or investment conversation, the content day when a customer finally tells the story you've been trying to capture, the testimonial filmed at your facility, the commercial that needs to show your people and your actual environment, the workshop where the marketing team finally gets on the same page.

Those moments benefit from something a video call can't fully reproduce.

Proximity.

That's one reason Wisconsin-based businesses have a unique opportunity when they work with a Wisconsin-based Fractional CMO: the flexibility of a modern virtual executive, without losing the ability to put your CMO in the room when being there matters.

Virtual doesn't have to mean distant

The right environment for the work, not a rule about where to sit.

I don't believe executives need to sit in an office five days a week to lead effectively. Research backs the value of hybrid work. But there's another side to the equation: some of the best work still happens in person.

−33%employee attrition with no performance loss, 2 days/week WFH · 1,612-employee randomized study
+15–20%more ideas generated by in-person vs. virtual teams in creative tasks · Stanford / Columbia research

So it isn't a choice between virtual and in-person. It's choosing the right environment for the work.

Zoom for what Zoom does well. In-person for what deserves the room.

Let's talk about what's next

The research behind purposeful proximity

Remote work has created enormous flexibility for modern businesses, and the evidence doesn't suggest that every meeting needs to happen in person: research on hybrid work has shown that flexible arrangements can preserve performance while improving retention and satisfaction. At the same time, research on creative collaboration has found meaningful differences in certain kinds of work, including a Stanford/Columbia study finding in-person teams generated 15–20% more ideas than virtual teams during creative idea-generation tasks, with a related field experiment finding in-person teams generated more, and more original, ideas. The takeaway isn't that businesses should abandon virtual work. It's that different work benefits from different environments, and strategic marketing leadership includes knowing the difference.
